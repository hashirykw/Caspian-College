About the college

The Caspian College for Science & Commerce is an intermediate college in Federal B Area, Karachi, teaching Classes XI and XII across the science and commerce streams. The campus sits at C-31, Block 13 — within reach of Water Pump, Ancholi, Gulberg and much of central Karachi.

What follows on this page is written to be useful before you have spoken to anyone: how intermediate is structured in Karachi, what each group leads to and forecloses, what the admission sequence looks like, and the specific questions worth asking at the office counter.

Details that change from one session to the next — which groups are running, the current fee schedule, shift timings, whether a van covers your area — are not printed here. Those come from the college directly, in writing, at admission. Anything else would be guesswork dressed up as information.

02

Science and commerce

Intermediate in Karachi is examined by the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi, and each stream splits into fixed groups with set subject combinations. The board decides the combinations; a college decides which of them it runs.

Science

Four groups

Three compulsory subjects and three electives, with a practical examination in each science subject.

Ask the office  which of these groups are running in the session you are joining.

Commerce

I.Com

Accounting is the subject that decides how comfortable ACCA, CA or a BBA feels two years from now. It is the one to ask about in detail.

Ask the office  which electives complete the I.Com set here.

*Subject combinations follow the Karachi board's intermediate scheme of studies, which is revised periodically. Confirm your exact subject list with the college, and check the current scheme before you commit to a group.

Choosing a group is the real decision

Choosing a college matters. Choosing the wrong group matters more — it is very hard to undo once board registration is filed, and it quietly closes degrees you may want in two years.

04

Admissions

The sequence below is the standard one at private intermediate colleges in Karachi. The Caspian College confirms its own dates, assessment format and charges directly — use this to know what is coming and what to ask at each step.

  • 01
    Call, then visit

    Ask to come during teaching hours rather than after them. An empty building tells you nothing. Bring the student — the useful questions come from them, not from you.

  • 02
    Submit your documents

    Matric result card, school leaving certificate, B-Form or CNIC, guardian CNIC and passport photographs. Ask for originals back the same day.

  • 03
    Assessment or counselling, where it applies

    Many colleges run a short placement test or a group-choice discussion. Ask whether one applies here, what it covers, and whether the result affects your group.

  • 04
    Pay, and get it in writing

    An itemised receipt and a written fee schedule for the year. If a charge is not on that schedule in August, it should not appear in March.

  • 05
    Board registration

    The college files your intermediate registration with the board. Keep your documents safe — they are needed again when examination forms are submitted.

*Admission windows follow the matric result announcement, so they move every year. Ask for this session's dates rather than relying on last year's.

Fees — and the questions worth asking

We are not publishing a fee table here, because a wrong number costs a family more than no number at all. Fees are confirmed by the college in writing at admission. What we can do is tell you exactly what to ask, so nothing lands on you in March.

Ask about Why it matters
Admission charge A one-time charge in the first year at most colleges. Confirm whether it repeats in Part-II, and whether it is refundable if you withdraw before classes begin.
Monthly tuition Ask whether it differs by group — science groups usually cost more than commerce — and whether it is payable monthly, per term, or annually.
What is inside it Laboratory, journal, library, test and activity charges. The question that matters is which of these are already covered and which arrive as a separate slip later.
Board examination fee Set by the board, not the college, and paid separately at examination form time. It should never be presented as a college charge.
Entry-test preparation If MDCAT or ECAT coaching is offered, ask whether it sits inside tuition or is billed on top — and whether it runs in college hours or in the evening.
Books and uniform Usually bought by the family. Ask for the list at admission, and whether it must be bought from a particular shop.
Transport Whether a van covers your area at all, what it costs, and whether the route is confirmed before or after you have paid.
Concessions Merit awards, sibling discounts and need-based concessions. Ask how they are assessed, and ask privately.

“Get the year's full fee schedule on paper before you pay anything. A college that will not put it in writing has told you what you needed to know.”

Take this list with you, or send it ahead so the office can answer in one message rather than five.

06

Visiting the campus

Half an hour on site tells you more than any website, this one included. Here is what to actually look at while you are there.

  • —
    Sit at the back of a running class

    Can you hear the teacher from there? Can the student in that seat see the board? That single test answers more than any stated class size.

  • —
    Ask to see a marked test paper

    Not a topper's. Any recent one. Look for whether a teacher actually wrote on it, and how long ago it was returned.

  • —
    Walk into the laboratories

    If you are joining a science group, look for apparatus that shows signs of use and journals that are current, not blank.

  • —
    Count the machines in the computer room

    For ICS this is the whole question. Ask how many students are in a practical session and how many machines work that day.

  • —
    Ask how parents are kept informed

    Reports, meetings, or a phone call when something slips — and how often, in writing.

  • —
    Check the practical details

    Prayer arrangements, break timings, the canteen, and how the students already there get home.
